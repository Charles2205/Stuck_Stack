# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Stuck Stack serves attendees, helpers, and organisers at live tech events. Attendees use it during a conference while moving between talks, hallway conversations, tables, and clinics. Their job is to name one blocker clearly, find someone nearby who can help, and leave with fewer unresolved problems. Organisers use it during the event to spot demand clusters and turn scattered attendee pain into pop-up clinics.

## Product Purpose

Stuck Stack turns event networking into a live problem-solving board. Success means attendees can join an event quickly, post or support a blocker, offer help, claim a short help slot, and see blockers move toward resolution. For organisers, success means the dashboard reveals what the room is struggling with and gives practical clinic suggestions they can act on immediately.

## Brand Personality

Helpful, direct, and pragmatic. The product voice should feel like a capable event-floor facilitator: concise, action-oriented, and warm without hype. It should make asking for help feel normal and make offering help feel lightweight.

## Anti-references

Avoid vague social-networking patterns, profile-heavy matchmaking, broad community feeds, marketing-style hero pages inside task flows, and decorative dashboards that hide the next action. The product should not feel like a generic SaaS landing page, a noisy conference app, or an overbuilt CRM.

## Design Principles

Prioritise the next useful action: every screen should make it obvious how to join, post a blocker, offer help, or act on a cluster.

Make pain legible at a glance: blockers, counts, helper availability, and status need strong scanning hierarchy.

Keep participation low-friction: event joining, sharing, and claiming help should work in seconds on a phone.

Give organisers operational clarity: suggestions should be specific enough to turn into a table, clinic, or announcement.

Respect the live-event context: assume crowded rooms, mobile screens, partial attention, and users who need readable controls quickly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for text contrast, focus visibility, semantic controls, labels, and keyboard access. Avoid color-only status communication, support reduced motion, keep touch targets comfortable, and ensure the join/share flow works for users scanning a QR code on mobile.
